What is Vatican Silk — really?                       Before we compare, clarify this first                       Vatican Silk is not a real silk. It is a               Surat               textile market trade name            for a poly-viscose blend fabric — typically 85% viscose + 15% polyester. The name was coined by fabric manufacturers in Gujarat to give it a premium-sounding identity. It has a silky drape and shine without the price or delicacy of actual silk. Many catalogues and wholesalers now use the name freely. Knowing this helps you pitch it accurately to your retailer buyers.                            Wholesaler alert                        Vatican Silk is an aspirational fabric name. Buyers love it because it sounds premium. Retailers love it because it sells at a mid-to-high price point but costs less than silk. This is your margin opportunity — as long as you are honest about the composition in your catalogue. Request 2–3 pieces from different designs before any bulk commitment. Inspect: fabric weight and GSM, colour fastness (ask to wet a corner and see if colour transfers), stitching quality (all seams should be double-stitched, no loose threads), size accuracy across the size run (M through XXL must be consistent), and how the garment looks after one machine wash. Sample red flag: If the sample quality is noticeably better than production-run quality, that's a known bait-and-switch practice. Cross-check samples against a mid-batch production piece if possible.                     4                    Understand MOQ structure and negotiate smartly                    MOQ in Ahmedabad varies widely: readymade catalogue wholesalers may offer 6–12 pieces per design, while direct manufacturers typically require 50–200 pieces per design for printed            kurtis            and 24–100 pieces for embroidered styles. MOQ is not fixed — it is a starting position. If you're a new buyer, ask for a "trial order" at 30–50% of their stated MOQ. Manufacturers who want long-term buyers will often accommodate. For complex embroidery or custom prints, expect higher MOQs (100–500 pieces) due to setup costs.                     Negotiation tip: Offer to pay 40–50% upfront and the rest on dispatch. This reduces the manufacturer's risk and gives you leverage to negotiate down the MOQ.                     5                    Ask the 10 key questions (see below)                    When meeting a manufacturer, have a structured conversation — not just price talk.
